# Liposomal copper peptides, then a NAD+ experiment

Status: draft

Groundwork for two unfinished cosmetics. Neither is a SKU, a price, or a page in the launch storefront. The launch catalog stays the one in [pricing-and-products.md](pricing-and-products.md): one Copper Tripeptide-1 serum and two GHK-Cu powder packs. This draft does not change the reseller decision in [storefront.md](storefront.md) or the claim rules in [brand-voice.md](brand-voice.md). It is not a formula, a process, or legal advice.

Voice for any future public sentence is the same biologic-hacker salesman: name the thing, name the boundary, then one next step. Until a formula sheet and a claim sheet exist, the next step is "not for sale."

## Two objects, in order

**L1 — Liposomal multi-copper peptide.** A finished cosmetic in development. More than one copper peptide, in a liposomal vehicle. The peptides are not chosen here. Copper Tripeptide-1 is the only copper peptide this house has already specified. Any second peptide is unnamed until the formula sheet prints its INCI and its percent.

**L2 — Nanoliposomal copper peptide or peptides, with nanoliposomal NAD+.** A later experiment. Two active families in one finished cosmetic, each in a nanoliposomal vehicle. L2 does not start until L1 has a formula sheet that names every copper peptide. NAD+ is not added to L1 to make L1 more interesting.

L1 and L2 are different products. "Liposomal" and "nanoliposomal" are different words. A page may use one of them only when the formula sheet says which, and only when the method that justifies "nano" is named beside it. A smaller-sounding word is not a stronger claim.

## What these are allowed to be

A cosmetic for the look and feel of skin, same claim class as the launch serum: `cosmetic_appearance`.

A format fact is allowed: water-based serum, liposomal vehicle or nanoliposomal vehicle, each peptide named, each percent printed, lot certificate linked. Appearance lines stay the launch set: look of fine lines, softer-looking texture, moisturized feel. New adjectives wait for counsel.

Identity facts are allowed: INCI, common name, CAS when the certificate or the formula sheet carries it, method, purity, lot. For NAD+, the INCI is confirmed from the formula sheet before it is printed. This draft does not guess it.

## What these are not

- Not a drug, and not a story about what a liposome does to a cell.
- Not a penetration, absorption, bioavailability, or "reaches the dermis" claim. The vehicle is named. The route into living skin is not.
- Not a blend nickname standing in for a percent. The Ordinary lesson in [ghk-cu-history.md](ghk-cu-history.md) still applies: a stack name and a stated percent of each peptide are different facts. If the bottle contains three copper peptides, the label has three lines.
- Not a place to attach collagen, energy, DNA, gene, inflammation, wound, or hair language to the SKU. Those words may appear on a science note only as labels for what a paper measured, with the evidence class in the same sentence.
- Not an instruction to mix liposomes, load an active, or pick a lipid ratio. Development detail of that kind stays off this repo until a later design is explicitly about the partner's disclosed formula sheet, and even then it is identity and specification, not a bench procedure.
- Not a raw-powder SKU. Liposomes are a finished vehicle. The formulator powder catalog does not gain a liposomal variant in this draft.
- Not public checkout. No Shopify product, no price, no "coming soon" buy button.

NAD+ adds a second refusal. Oral, intravenous, and cellular-energy stories about NAD+ do not transfer to a cosmetic on intact skin. A paper in cells, animals, or people is labeled with that setting. It is not a result for this bottle. GHK Labs has not run a finished-product test of L1 or L2. When one exists, it gets its own line, separate from ingredient papers.

## How a future page would earn trust

Same order as [storefront-copy.md](storefront-copy.md), plus one line for the vehicle.

1. Category first: finished cosmetic serum. Not a research chemical. Not a supplement.
2. One inspectable fact before any skin adjective: a named peptide and its printed percent, or the lot.
3. Vehicle as a fact: "liposomal" or "nanoliposomal," with the characterizing method beside "nano" if that word is used. No performance adjective on the particle.
4. Seller versus laboratory: GHK Labs sells. The manufacturer writes the certificate. The house does not imply it built the liposome.
5. Each active in its own sentence. Copper peptides do not borrow NAD+ papers, and NAD+ does not borrow copper-peptide papers.
6. One ask, and only after the claim sheet exists. Before that, the page is a lab note with no shop link.

Representative-lot language still applies. The certificate on the page is the current released lot unless the partner feed guarantees the unit in the order.

## Development record, before any copy

Nothing in this list is invented to fill a gap. A blank stays blank.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Each copper peptide, INCI and common name | Yes | Yes, plus any peptide L1 did not already name |
| Stated percent of each peptide, from the formula sheet | Yes | Yes |
| Vehicle word: liposomal or nanoliposomal | Yes | Yes, and the method for "nano" |
| NAD+ identity and stated percent | No | Yes |
| Who manufactures and who writes the certificate | Yes | Yes |
| Finished-goods lot, identity method, purity or assay | Yes, to sell | Yes, to sell |

If the formula is made by the same unnamed supply partner as the launch serum, fulfillment and COA rules in [fulfillment-model.md](fulfillment-model.md) apply unchanged: manufacturer's certificate, partner unnamed, GHK Labs is seller of record. If someone later proposes that this house manufacture L1 or L2, that is a new sourcing design. It is not slipped into this note.

## Catalog posture

Launch storefront: unchanged.

L1: in development. A design row and, later, a private lab note. No nav item.

L2: an experiment that waits on L1's formula sheet. It is not a sequel promised on the L1 page. Combining two fashionable actives is how blend names get louder than the evidence. The fun of the experiment is keeping the two actives in separate sentences while they share one bottle.

Public science copy, when it is written, is a separate draft. It does not ship inside this groundwork. Likely rooms, in the house voice:

- Laboratory, animal, human, review, and "this bottle," same five classes as the serum pillar.
- One room for the copper peptide that is actually in the bottle. A second peptide gets its own room, not a shared adjective.
- One room for the vehicle word, saying what was measured (a particle description on a spec sheet) and what was not (a clinical delivery result).
- For L2 only, one room for NAD+, opened by the route and the setting of the paper, then closed before any sentence about the serum.

## Open questions

1. Which copper peptides are in L1, beyond the decision that Copper Tripeptide-1 is the only one already specified?
2. Is the vehicle liposomal, nanoliposomal, or undecided? What measurement, if any, does the formula sheet already have?
3. Who makes L1, and is the sourcing model still reseller?
4. Does "multi-copper" mean more than one peptide species, or more than one copper complex of the same peptide? The label has to pick one meaning.
5. For L2, is NAD+ in the same particle as the peptide or in a second population in the same bottle? The label should not say "joint" until that is known. "Joint" in this draft means one finished cosmetic, not one shared mechanism.
6. Which appearance lines, if any, counsel will allow beyond the launch set?
7. Where the lab note lives before launch: unpublished design only, or a `/journal` page that cannot be mistaken for a product.

## Out of scope

Lipid choice, loading steps, particle targets, prices, Shopify products, ads, and any sentence that tells a reader to apply NAD+ or a copper peptide for energy, repair, or a disease. Those wait for a later design that has a formula sheet to point at.

## Acceptance

Not accepted. This note is groundwork so L1 and L2 are not written into the launch catalog by accident. Accepting it later would accept the boundaries and the open questions, not a formula and not a claim.
